# Staying Safe: A Practical Guide to Avoiding Today's Scams

## Three-Part Course for Seniors

**Audience:** Seniors and older adults

**Recommended Length**
- Session 1: 45–60 minutes

---

# Course Philosophy

## The Cat-and-Mouse Game

Scammers never stop innovating.

Every year they invent new stories, take advantage of new technology, and exploit current events. They may use artificial intelligence to clone voices, fake text messages that look genuine, or create convincing websites and emails.

This is a constant cat-and-mouse game. As law enforcement and technology companies block one type of scam, criminals invent another.

Fortunately, while the stories change, the tactics rarely do.

Whether someone pretends to be:

- your bank,
- Medicare,
- your grandchild,
- the IRS,
- Amazon,
- Microsoft,
- or even a close friend,

they almost always rely on the same psychological tricks:

- creating urgency,
- creating fear,
- creating excitement,
- demanding secrecy,
- asking for unusual forms of payment.

Instead of trying to memorize every possible scam, you'll learn to recognize these warning signs. Those skills will remain useful even as scams continue to evolve.

---

# The One Rule to Remember

Whenever someone wants you to act immediately:

> **STOP → THINK → VERIFY**

This simple habit can prevent most scams.

# Session 3 — Staying Safe Every Day

## Goal

Build everyday habits that reduce the risk of becoming a victim.

### Topics

1. Safe Online Shopping
2. Social Media Scams
3. Password Safety
4. Two-Factor Authentication
5. Protecting Personal Information
6. What To Do If You Think You've Been Scammed
7. Reporting Fraud
8. The Golden Rules of Scam Prevention

### Interactive Activity

"Scam or Legitimate?"

Review 10–15 real examples.

For each one, ask:

- What makes this suspicious?
- Which warning signs do you see?
- What should you do next?

Encourage discussion before revealing the answer.

### Key Takeaways

Participants leave with practical habits they can use every day rather than simply remembering individual scams.

# Universal Warning Signs

Almost every scam contains one or more of these:

✅ Urgency

> "Act now!"

---

✅ Fear

> "Your account has been compromised."

---

✅ Excitement

> "You've won a prize!"

---

✅ Secrecy

> "Don't tell anyone."

---

✅ Unusual Payment Requests

- Gift cards
- Wire transfers
- Cryptocurrency
- Payment apps

---

# The Golden Rules

1. Slow down.
2. Verify independently.
3. Never trust caller ID alone.
4. Never pay with gift cards.
5. Never share passwords.
6. Don't click unexpected links.
7. Ask a trusted family member.
8. When in doubt, hang up.

---

# Final Message

Technology will continue to change.

Artificial intelligence will make scams look and sound more convincing.

New scams will appear every year.

But the criminals' goal never changes:

They want you to act before you have time to think.

Your best defense is not having the newest computer or smartphone.

Your best defense is developing one simple habit:

> **STOP. THINK. VERIFY.**

That habit has protected people for decades—and will continue to protect them no matter what new scam comes next.
