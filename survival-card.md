# Scam Survival Card

## Keep Near Your Phone or Computer

---

# STOP • THINK • VERIFY

Before you send money, click a link, or share personal information:

## STOP

Don't let anyone rush you.

## THINK

Does this request make sense?

## VERIFY

Contact the person or organization using a phone number or website **you already know**, not the one they gave you.

---

# The Five Biggest Red Flags

If any of these happen, slow down immediately.

☐ "You must act right now."

☐ "Don't tell anyone."

☐ "Pay with gift cards, cryptocurrency, or a wire transfer."

☐ "Click this link immediately."

☐ "Give me your password or verification code."

Even one of these is reason to pause. Two or more almost always indicate a scam.

---

# Before You Send Money, Ask Yourself

□ Was I expecting this call, email, or text?

□ Do I personally know who contacted me?

□ Have I verified this independently?

□ Am I being pressured?

□ Have I talked to a trusted friend or family member?

If you answer **No** to any of the first three questions—or **Yes** to either of the last two—**don't act yet.**

---

# Remember

## Banks will NOT:

• Ask for your online banking password.

• Ask you to move money to a "safe account."

• Ask you to buy gift cards.

---

## Government agencies will NOT:

• Demand immediate payment over the phone.

• Accept payment with gift cards.

• Threaten immediate arrest during an unexpected phone call.

---

## Technology companies will NOT:

• Call you unexpectedly to report viruses on your computer.

• Ask for remote access to your computer unless **you** requested support.

---

# If Someone Claims to Be Family

Hang up.

Call your family member directly using the number you already have.

Never send money until you've confirmed the story.

---

# If You've Already Shared Information

Don't panic—but act quickly.

1. Contact your bank or credit card company.
2. Change any passwords that may have been exposed.
3. Tell a trusted family member or friend.
4. Report the incident to the appropriate authorities.

The sooner you act, the more options you may have.

---

# The Golden Rules

✓ It's okay to say **No.**

✓ It's okay to hang up.

✓ It's okay to take your time.

✓ It's okay to ask for help.

No legitimate organization will object if you take time to verify who they are.

---

# One Last Thought

Scammers are constantly changing their stories.

Today it may be a fake bank call.

Tomorrow it may be an AI-generated voice that sounds exactly like your grandchild.

The technology will change.

The tactics will change.

But one thing never changes:

Scammers want you to react before you think.

Whenever something feels urgent, emotional, secretive, or simply "not quite right,"

# STOP → THINK → VERIFY

That simple habit is your strongest defense against fraud.
